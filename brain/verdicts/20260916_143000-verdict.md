## Critical Side Findings

### 1. Hysteresis at 50/50 May Be a Design Flaw
The sniper's hysteresis thresholds are both set to 50. This means:
- If BTC score > 50: BEARISH exits are suppressed (can't close LONGs)
- If BTC score < 50: BULLISH exits are suppressed (can't close SHORTs)
- The oscillator spends most time on one side of 50, so the sniper almost never gets permission to act
- This likely explains the "0 trades closed" finding
- **Recommendation:** Consider asymmetric thresholds (e.g., LONG=55, SHORT=45) to create a usable dead zone

### 2. Sample Size Concern
The continuum-matched trade analysis only covered 510 trades out of 5,081 total. This is because most trades don't have BTC continuum data within the matching window. The 73% WR claim for >6h trades comes from just 48 trades — a small sample.

### 3. Score Direction Claims Are Direction-Dependent
The plan says "score falling = 64% WR" as a general statement, but the reality is heavily direction-dependent. Score falling is great for SHORTs (79.5% WR) but terrible for LONGs (43.0% WR). The plan should specify which direction this applies to.

### 4. Plan Data May Be Stale
Several WR figures in the plan don't match current data:
- 1-6 hour WR: plan says 45%, actual is 53%
- Score 60-80 LONG: plan says 79%, actual is 65.8%
This suggests the plan was written at a different time with different data, or used different methodology.
